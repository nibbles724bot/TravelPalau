Snorkeling highlights from Shark City to Jellyfish Lake

  • Shark City

    Look for reef sharks from the surface. Choose an operator that does not feed or bait wildlife and keep your distance. Google Maps

  • Giant Clam City

    Look for giant clams and their colorful mantles in shallow water. Keep hands, feet and fins clear. Google Maps

  • Big Drop Off

    The reef drops steeply into deep water. Look for tropical fish and possibly turtles. Let your guide choose a snorkeling area based on currents. Google Maps

  • Milky Way

    Fine white sediment gives the lagoon its milky turquoise color. Follow your guide and posted rules. Google Maps

  • Jellyfish Lake

    Obtain the Jellyfish Lake permit and confirm the lake is open that day. On sunny days golden jellyfish often move east in the morning and west in the afternoon. Follow the guide to where they are that day. Where fins are allowed, kick slowly, keep a gap and avoid contact. Google Maps

Scuba sites and training

  • Blue Corner

    A renowned outer reef point with strong currents and schooling fish. Google Maps

  • Blue Holes

    Openings in the reef lead into a cavern known for its light shafts. A qualified scuba guide should assess cavern access. Google Maps

  • Big Drop Off

    Known for its reef wall. Entry points depend on sea conditions and the activity. Google Maps

Certified scuba trips check your Open Water or equivalent card before departure. If you do not have it, stick with snorkeling or ask separately about instructor-led Discover Scuba Diving. Operators assess access to Blue Corner, Blue Holes and Big Drop Off based on certification, experience and sea conditions.

Allow roughly 3–4 days for Open Water. USD 550 is a rough planning figure, with some courses costing more. Certification follows successful training and assessment. Ask what the quote includes: permits, tanks, weights, lunch, equipment and Nitrox.

Carp Island overnight

Carp Island is also called Southern Cross Island. Allow roughly one hour by speedboat from Koror, depending on the boat and conditions. For a stargazing overnight, confirm accommodation, round-trip transfers, dinner and your onward schedule. Cloud cover and the moon affect visibility. Bring insect repellent.

Snorkeling and trail safety

Wear a life jacket, follow your guide and stay in water suited to your ability. Wear a rash guard for sun coverage and avoid stepping on coral.

Wear shoes with good grip on the waterfall trail. Turn back if water levels rise or the trail closes.

Practical travel information

  • Sunscreen rules

    Palau bans reef-toxic sunscreen. Check the official ingredient list and choose a compliant zinc oxide or titanium dioxide product. Pack a spare bottle. Palau sunscreen regulations

  • Palau Pledge

    On entry, sign the passport pledge to respect Palau’s environment, culture and communities. Read the pledge

  • Emergency and medical help

    Call 911 for emergencies. Belau National Hospital is in Meyuns, Koror. Its emergency department is at +680 488 2558. Serious cases may require evacuation. Government directory

  • Internet, eSIM and SIM

    Mobile coverage tends to be better in Koror and Airai, with weaker or no service offshore. Ask your hotel about Wi‑Fi for work. PNCC offers prepaid eSIM in Koror and physical SIMs in Koror and Airai. Download maps, your entry QR code and bookings before heading out. PNCC visitor plans and shops

  • Plugs and voltage

    Palau uses Type A/B outlets at 120 V, 60 Hz. Taiwan phone and laptop chargers with the same plug and a 100–240 V rating usually fit directly. Check appliances rated only for 110 V. Palau consulate information

  • Credit cards and US dollars

    Major credit cards are widely accepted at hotels and tourist businesses. Some small shops are cash-only. Carry small US dollar bills and ask operators about card acceptance or fees. Canada travel advice

  • Underwater camera rental

    Ask Splash about underwater camera rental, availability, housing, memory cards, deposit and how images are transferred. Splash equipment rentals

Booking and entry checklist

  1. Book flights and four nights of accommodation.Confirm arrival on Day 1 and departure on Day 5. Give the hotel your flight number and arrival time to arrange both transfers.
  2. Book water activities and the island tour.Book the Day 2 boat first, then book the Day 3 car and confirm the Day 4 lake backup or guided snorkeling outside the cave. Before paying, check weather cancellation and rescheduling terms, transfers, meals, equipment and admission. Request a Mandarin-speaking guide if needed.
  3. Prepare your entry documents.Taiwanese passport holders should check that their passport has at least six months of validity and carry a return ticket. Submit the Palau Entry Form within 72 hours before departure and save the QR code. Check the rules for other passports. Palau entry requirements · Palau Entry Form
  4. Pack your gear.Pack a rash guard, dry bag, shoes with good grip, reusable water bottle and small US dollar bills. Check whether any travel insurance covers snorkeling and kayaking.
