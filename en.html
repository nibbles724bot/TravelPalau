Palau Visitors Authority
Ten highlights in Palau

  1. Rock Islands

    Emerald islands and shifting shades of blue make this mixed natural and cultural World Heritage site one of Palau’s signature landscapes.

    Day 2 itinerary

  2. Scenic flight

    See the Seventy Islands from a small plane. Ask about 40–60 minute sightseeing flights.

  3. Milky Way

    A milky turquoise lagoon with natural white limestone mud on its floor.

    Day 2 itinerary

  4. Jellyfish Lake

    You may be able to snorkel among golden jellyfish. Check whether the lake is open and ask about recent sightings before setting out.

    Optional on Day 2

  5. Long Beach

    A white sandbar emerges at low tide and offers a scenic photo stop. Landing depends on the tide.

    Day 2, tide permitting

  6. Big Drop Off

    A coral wall drops sharply from shallow reefs into deep blue water. Ask about tours suitable for current sea conditions.

  7. German Channel

    A well-known manta ray site for experienced divers. Sightings vary.

  8. Kayangel

    A sparsely populated northern atoll with undeveloped scenery. Boat access depends on sea conditions.

  9. Kayaking in Nikko Bay

    Paddle past limestone islands, mangroves and quiet lagoons. Ask your operator for the Nikko Bay route.

    Optional on Day 4

  10. Ngardmau Waterfall

    Follow a tropical forest trail to the waterfall for a look at Palau’s inland scenery.

    Day 3 itinerary

Snorkeling and trail safety

Wear a life jacket, follow your guide and stay in water suited to your ability. Wear a rash guard for sun coverage and avoid stepping on coral.

Wear shoes with good grip on the waterfall trail. Turn back if water levels rise or the trail closes.

Booking and entry checklist

  1. Book flights and four nights of accommodation.Confirm arrival on Day 1 and departure on Day 5. Give the hotel your flight number and arrival time to arrange both transfers.
  2. Book water activities and the island tour.Book the Day 2 boat first, then the Day 3 car and Day 4 kayak tour. Before paying, check weather cancellation and rescheduling terms, transfers, meals, equipment and admission. Request a Mandarin-speaking guide if needed.
  3. Prepare your entry documents.Taiwanese passport holders should check that their passport has at least six months of validity and carry a return ticket. Submit the Palau Entry Form within 72 hours before departure and save the QR code. Check the rules for other passports. Palau entry requirements · Palau Entry Form
  4. Pack your gear.Pack a rash guard, dry bag, shoes with good grip, reusable water bottle and small US dollar bills. Check whether any travel insurance covers snorkeling and kayaking.
